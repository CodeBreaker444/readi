Operations is where missions move from planned to completed. It has four views of the same missions, in the sidebar under **Operations**.

| View | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Mission Table** | Full list of missions with filters and details |
| **Daily Board** | Drag-and-drop board: Scheduled, In Progress, Done |
| **Calendar** | Schedule by week, month or list, and add missions |
| **Flight Requests** | Requests waiting for a decision (internal and external) |

Admins, OPMs and Super Admins also see **Email Notifications** to choose which mission emails are sent.

---

## Creating a mission

Click **Add Mission** (Calendar) or **New** (Mission Table). The **New Mission** screen has these parts:

1. **Client:** who the mission is for.
2. **Operation type and drone system:**

| Operation type | What it needs |
|---|---|
| **Open** | Pilot flies with the remote control. No mission plan |
| **PDRA** | Flies under a risk analysis. A mission plan is required |
| **STS-01 / STS-02** | Standard scenarios. Same as Open, no mission plan |

3. **Mission details:** category, type, location, date and time, pilot, procedure and notes.
4. **Recurring missions:** choose the days of the week and an end date to create the whole series at once.

### Weather for the mission

Once you choose a start date and time, a **Weather** badge appears under it showing the conditions icon, whether the hour is good, marginal or no-go, and a score out of 100 (a cross for no-go, `--` for night).

Hover it to see the conditions, wind and gusts against the drone's limits, rain, cloud cover, visibility and temperature, and which drone models it was scored against.

- **Which location:** the mission's own location if you set one, otherwise the selected system's location. If neither has a location, add one to see the weather.
- **Which limits:** the selected system's drones. Without a drone model, generic limits are used and the badge says so.
- **Which hour:** the hour starting at the chosen start time, in your timezone (the same one the rest of the app uses). The details show the time range with the timezone.
- **How far ahead:** the forecast reaches 16 days. For a time in the past, or further ahead, the badge says so instead.

This is for planning only. It does not stop you from creating the mission.

### Systems you cannot pick

The list marks systems that are unavailable, with a reason:

- **Maintenance** or **Maintenance Due:** under maintenance, or due soon (review before scheduling)
- **Not Operational:** a component has expired. Creation is blocked
- **Dismissed:** administratively retired
- **SN Mismatch:** no system for that client has the serial number

### Companies using D-Flight

For Open, STS-01, STS-02 and PDRA missions you also draw the flight area on the map (a circle, or the plan's route for PDRA). See [D-Flight Mission Authorization](/docs/business-logic/dflight-authorization).

### Missions created by pilots

A pilot's mission goes to the OPMs of their department for approval before it appears on the Daily Board. See [Mission Approval (OPM)](/docs/business-logic/mission-approval).

## Daily Board

Each mission is a card in one of three columns: **Scheduled**, **In Progress** or **Done**. Drag a card to the next column to start or finish the mission.

Cards show the pilot, the system, and its maintenance state (**Maintenance OK**, **Alert**, **Due** or **In Maintenance**). Open a card for the timeline (planned, official start and end, actual start and end), people and equipment, and flight results.

Before a card can move, Readi checks that:

- The system is Operational and not in maintenance
- The pilot has filed today's declaration (below)
- The mission has OPM approval, if it needed it
- D-Flight has accepted it, if your company uses D-Flight
- You have set an **Authorization PIN**, which you enter to confirm the move

See [Mission Status Gates](/docs/platform-rules/mission-status-gates) and [Authorization Code (PIN)](/docs/platform-rules/authorization-code).

### Daily Pilot Declaration

Each day a pilot must confirm, before starting a mission, that they:

- are rested and not fatigued
- are physically and mentally fit
- are not under medication that could affect performance
- have reviewed the mission plan and area
- have inspected the equipment and drone systems

All items must be ticked before submitting.

### Procedure tasks

If the mission has a company procedure, its tasks appear on the card. Complete them before finishing the mission. When done the card shows "All tasks completed".

## Completing a mission

When a mission is finished, **Mission Complete** has three tabs:

| Tab | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Maintenance** | Record the flights, hours and days used on each component. Usage can be automatic or manual. It can only be recorded once per mission |
| **Flight Logs** | Attach the flight log, by uploading a file (.zip, .json, .xml) or fetching it from Control Center |
| **Post-Flight Data** | Result, notes, distance and other final details |

A component that reaches its maintenance limit shows "Maintenance is overdue". See the [Maintenance Module](/docs/business-logic/maintenance-module).

---

## Importing a flown mission

Use **Import mission** to add a flight that was already flown. It takes four steps: **Client**, **Log File** (upload a .gutma or .zip file, or pick a flight from Control Center), **Mission Data** and **Pilot**.

## Calendar

Shows missions as colored entries: Scheduled, In Progress and Completed. Switch between **Today**, **Week**, **Month** and **List**.

### Best Time to Fly

The **Best Time to Fly** tab next to the calendar shows the next 7 days as a grid of flight windows (06:00 to 22:00) and tells you when your drones can fly safely. It is scored against the limits of the drones you choose.

#### Choosing the location

The forecast is fetched for one location at a time. Open the location field to choose:

- **A system:** the forecast uses that system's own location.
- **Choose on map:** search for a place or drop a pin anywhere.
- **Crosshair button:** uses your current position.

No location is selected when you open the tab, and your choice is not kept after a refresh. **All fleet types** is selected to start with. If you pick a system that has no location, you are asked to choose one. Use the **x** in the location field to clear the location, for example to go back from a map point to a single system's location.

#### Choosing drones

Drones are your active drone components that have a serial number, grouped under their system. A warning icon on a chip, and a note under the drones, mean one or more drones have no model set and use generic limits. Each chip is one system; if a system has more than one drone, the chip shows the count (for example **×2**) and choosing it selects all of them.

- **No location chosen yet:** picking a system selects its drones, and the system's location is used. If that system has no location, you are asked to choose one.
- **A system supplies the location:** only that system's drones can be chosen. Drones of other systems are greyed out, and a grey note under the drones explains why. Click the chosen system again to deselect it and clear its location; the other systems can then be chosen.
- **A location picked on the map:** you can choose drones from several systems.
- **Several drones selected:** a window is only green if every one of them can fly it (the strictest limits apply).
- **Many drones:** the first few show as chips; use the **+N** list to search by system, drone, model or serial number.

<Note>
Not seeing a drone? It needs to be on an active system, be a **Drone** component, and have a serial number. A drone with no model set is still listed (with a warning icon) and uses generic limits until a model is set on its component.
</Note>

#### Reading the grid

Each cell is one window (length set by **Slot length**, 1 to 4 hours) with:

- the average wind, the peak gust and the total rain
- a weather icon for the conditions
- a large number: the score out of 100, a cross when a limit is exceeded, or `--` at night

Use **Show** to switch the large number between the overall score, wind, gusts and rain. A window that is not green names what is holding it back (for example **Wind**) right in the cell.

**Click or tap a window** for the full detail: the conditions, the day and time with its timezone, the score, and wind, gusts, rain, rain chance, cloud cover, visibility and temperature next to the limits they were scored against, plus which drone models those limits come from. The **How to read this** button next to the legend explains every color, icon and number.

On a narrow screen (a phone, or a narrow window) the grid shows one day at a time: pick the day from the row of days above it.

| Color | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Green** | Good to fly: safe margin on every limit |
| **Amber** | Marginal: within 25% of a limit, fly with care |
| **Red** | No-go: a drone limit is exceeded |
| **Grey** | Night: outside daylight, never recommended |

The best upcoming window is outlined in violet with a star. All times are in your own timezone (shown at the bottom of the grid), even if the location is in another one, and the rows cover the hours that have daylight. A window that has already started, even by a few minutes, is dimmed and is never recommended: at 11:45 the 10:00 to 12:00 window is out, and the next candidate starts at 12:00. The **Retrieved** time next to **Show** says how old the forecast is, for example "5 min ago". The grid keeps itself up to date while the tab is open: windows drop out as they start, and the forecast reloads in the background every hour and when the day changes.

#### Weather icons

| Icon | Conditions |
|---|---|
| Sun | Clear sky, mostly clear |
| Sun behind cloud | Partly cloudy |
| Cloud | Overcast |
| Fog | Fog |
| Light rain | Drizzle |
| Rain | Rain, showers |
| Snow | Snow |
| Storm | Thunderstorm |
| Moon | Night. It also shows the night's conditions, for example a cloudy or rainy night |

#### How the score works

Every hour is checked against the limits of the selected drones: wind, gusts, rain, visibility and temperature. The weakest of these decides the result. A window is only as good as its worst hour, and the score is that hour's score. A window containing night hours is never recommended.

A drone that is not waterproof (rain limit 0) is grounded by anything above a trace of rain.

#### Limits

Limits belong to the **drone model**, so drones of the same model share them. They start from a manufacturer default; use the gear icon on a system to set your own (if a system has drones of different models, the gear lists each model so you can edit them all): maximum wind, gusts and rain, minimum visibility, and the temperature range.

The forecast is advisory only. Always check official aviation weather before you fly.

## Flight Requests

Has two tabs:

- **Internal:** missions created by your pilots that need OPM approval. Approve, or deny with an optional reason. Filter by All, Pending, Approved or Denied.
- **External:** requests from outside systems and clients (see [DCC Integration](/docs/business-logic/dcc-integration) and [Client Portal](/docs/modules/client-portal)). Requests are counted as New, Acknowledged or Assigned. An OPM can **Move to Planning** (assign it to a planning mission and a plan), or deny it.
